## 🎬 Introduction

**Cold Case Vegas** is an interactive, AI-powered detective game where **you** are the interrogator. Set against the neon-lit backdrop of the Las Vegas Strip, the game puts you face-to-face with suspects who think, react, and lie — powered by large language models.

Each case drops you into a crime scene with a victim, a handful of suspects, and a pile of evidence that doesn't quite add up. There are no multiple-choice options. No predetermined dialogue trees. You type your own questions, challenge alibis in your own words, and watch suspects squirm, deflect, or break under pressure — all driven by AI in real time.

The twist? **The suspicion meter is designed to mislead you.** It measures linguistic evasiveness, not guilt. An innocent person who's hiding a secret will score high. The real killer might stay perfectly calm. You have to think like a detective, not follow a number.

> *"The suspicion meter does not solve cases. It misleads them. Think like a detective, not an algorithm."*

## 🕹️ How to Play

### 1. Select a Case
From the main screen, choose one of the three available cases. Each card shows the case title, location, victim, and key stats. Click **OPEN CASE** to begin.

### 2. Read the Case Briefing
A detailed case file modal appears with:
- **Crime scene summary** — what happened and when
- **Initial evidence** — the first clues found at the scene
- **Suspect profiles** — who you'll be interrogating

Read everything carefully. This briefing is your foundation. Click **BEGIN INVESTIGATION** when ready.

### 3. Choose a Suspect
On the left side of the investigation screen, you'll see three suspect cards. Click any suspect to enter their interrogation room. You can switch between suspects freely at any time — each conversation is tracked separately.

### 4. Interrogate
Type your questions in the chat input at the bottom. There are no prompts or suggestions — you decide what to ask. Some effective strategies:

- 🕐 **Challenge their timeline** — *"You said you left at 11, but the keycard log shows 11:30. Explain."*
- 🔍 **Press on specifics** — *"Who else was on Floor 12 that night?"*
- 💣 **Introduce evidence** — *"We found the waste chute had been recently used. Care to comment?"*
- 🎭 **Confront contradictions** — *"That's not what Diana told us. She says you were arguing."*

### 5. Gather Clues
As you interrogate, clues appear in the **Clue Panel** on the right side of the screen. Clues are revealed when:
- You mention specific keywords related to the case evidence
- The suspect's response contains revealing information
- The AI detects a significant admission or contradiction

### 6. Make the Arrest
Once you're confident, click the **MAKE ARREST** button in the clue panel. Select which suspect you believe is the killer. The game will then reveal:
- Whether you arrested the correct person
- A dramatic AI-generated narrative explaining the full truth of what happened
- How the clues you gathered connected (or didn't) to the real killer

## 🧠 Game Mechanics Explained


### 📊 Suspicion Meter

Each suspect has a suspicion score (0–100) displayed on their card. This score is generated by an AI deception-analysis model that evaluates linguistic evasiveness in the suspect's responses.

| Score Range | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| 0 – 30 | Direct, specific, credible responses |
| 31 – 60 | Some hedging or vagueness detected |
| 61 – 100 | Highly evasive, contradictory, or deceptive patterns |

> ⚠️ **Important:** A high suspicion score does NOT mean someone is guilty. An innocent person hiding an affair, a debt, or a secret will also score high. The meter measures *how they talk*, not *whether they're the killer*. Use it as one data point, not as your verdict.

### 🔎 Clue Discovery

Clues are revealed through two systems working together:

1. **Keyword Rules** — Each case has a curated set of trigger keywords. When your question or the suspect's reply contains these keywords, a specific clue is unlocked. For example, mentioning "keycard" or "Floor 12" during Diana's interrogation might reveal evidence about her movements.

2. **AI Clue Extraction** — If no keyword rule is triggered, an AI model analyzes the exchange for investigative significance and may surface a new clue on its own.

Each clue can only be discovered once — the game tracks which clue IDs you've already found.

### 🧩 Intent Classification

Behind the scenes, the AI classifies each of your questions into one of four categories:

| Intent | Description | Effect on Suspect |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **ALIBI** | Asking about whereabouts and location | Suspect reinforces their stated timeline |
| **MOTIVE** | Asking about relationships, grudges, money | Suspect deflects from any suggestion of motive |
| **TIMELINE** | Asking about specific times and sequences | Suspect is precise about their stated times |
| **GENERAL** | Any other type of question | Neutral response behavior |

This classification shapes how the suspect AI responds, making conversations feel more natural and reactive.

### 📚 RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

Each case has its own knowledge base stored in a vector database (ChromaDB). When you ask a question, the system retrieves the most relevant case documents and feeds them to the suspect AI as context. This ensures suspects:
- Stay consistent with the case narrative
- Reference specific details, names, and events accurately
- Don't hallucinate facts that contradict the case story

## 🎯 Tips for Detectives

1. **Read the case file thoroughly** — Initial evidence contains critical details that can break alibis.
2. **Talk to every suspect** — Switching between suspects and cross-referencing their stories is essential.
3. **Be specific** — Mentioning specific times, names, and locations triggers more detailed (and revealing) responses.
4. **Don't trust the suspicion meter** — It measures evasiveness, not guilt. An innocent person hiding a secret scores high.
5. **Push through DEFENSIVE** — When a suspect goes defensive, keep pressing with evidence. That's when the cracks appear.
6. **Look for contradictions** — Compare what suspects say about each other. The lies are in the details.
7. **Use evidence as leverage** — Confronting suspects with evidence they don't expect you to have produces the best reactions.
